Grey bars are the shoulder and elbow, capped at 30 and 40 for every character because the simulation showed fast elbow moves overloading the shoulder. Shoulder peak is the highest load over all ten clips, standing pose, as a share of 2.94 N·m stall torque.

How the clips were made

Each character's gesture is planned by character.py, then played on a MuJoCo model of the ElRobot with real 55 g servos. Every servo runs a model of the STS3215 firmware: speed and acceleration ramp, position loop, 2.94 N·m and 4.71 rad/s motor limits. The arms show the joint angles that came out, at 30 frames per second.

What to look for

Same gesture, different settings. The trickster winds up and overshoots, the grump stops dead at each pose, the shy one barely moves and waits, and smooth never stops inside a gesture because it follows a minimum-jerk curve instead of jumping between poses.

Limits

Servo gain, rotor inertia and backlash are estimates; the real arm will differ a little. Joint directions depend on calibration, so a gesture can mirror on your arm (flip it in SIGN). Station and network delay are not in the clips.
